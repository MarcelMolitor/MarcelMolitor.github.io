1. Beyond the classical receptors

The endocannabinoid system is commonly introduced through two receptors, CB1 and CB2, and the endogenous lipid mediators anandamide and 2-arachidonoylglycerol. This framework is useful, but it does not encompass every biological interaction of these molecules. The broader endocannabinoidome includes related lipids, metabolic enzymes and additional molecular targets. Membership in this broader network does not imply that every target is a classical cannabinoid receptor.

Understanding this distinction matters because a single compound can affect several molecular systems. A biological response following exposure to a cannabinoid cannot automatically be attributed to CB1 or CB2. The task of receptor research is to establish which molecular interactions contribute to a particular response under defined conditions.

2. GPR55 and lipid signalling

GPR55 is a G protein-coupled receptor studied in connection with lysophosphatidylinositol (LPI). Its classification as a third cannabinoid receptor is not universally established. Cell-based studies have investigated responses involving calcium, RhoA and downstream signalling pathways. These measurements describe different stages of cellular communication and should not be treated as interchangeable.

A useful research question is whether two ligands produce different relative calcium and ERK responses in the same receptor-expressing model. Such a difference could motivate further investigation of functional selectivity. Alternative explanations, including differences in exposure and receptor expression, must also be considered. This is a proposed hypothesis, not a demonstrated property of arbitrary ligands.

Primary literature: Oka et al., 2007; Henstridge et al., 2009.

3. TRP channels

Transient receptor potential channels are ion channels rather than classical G protein-coupled cannabinoid receptors. Their activation can alter ion movement across membranes. TRPV1 is a prominent example of a channel that responds to diverse stimuli, including certain lipid mediators under appropriate conditions. Anandamide has been investigated in relation to vanilloid receptor-mediated responses.

Calcium measurements alone cannot establish whether a response originated from direct channel activation or from an indirect signalling pathway. Independent measurements and suitable controls are essential for mechanistic interpretation.

Primary literature: Zygmunt et al., 1999.

4. From signals to evidence

A receptor response in a cell model is not evidence of clinical efficacy by itself. Binding, functional activation, organism-level exposure and clinical benefit represent distinct questions. Artificial expression systems may produce responses that differ from those in native tissues. A rigorous interpretation therefore considers receptor density, concentration, time course and the availability of signalling partners.

Negative findings are informative when the experiment is capable of detecting the predicted response. Positive and negative controls help distinguish technical limitations from biological explanations. A well-defined hypothesis specifies the model, comparison and measurable endpoint before interpreting results.

5. The language of cells

Cells communicate through chemical and physical processes rather than a central command system. Receptors translate molecular interactions into changes in cellular activity. They are not simple binary switches: a ligand can alter the distribution of molecular states, influencing interactions with other proteins.

Membranes are dynamic environments. Lipid composition, protein binding and metabolism can affect the amount of a compound available at a target. Nominal concentration therefore need not equal free concentration. Affinity describes binding under defined conditions, whereas potency concerns the concentration required for a specified functional response. Neither alone establishes therapeutic usefulness.

Time is another important variable. A brief calcium signal and a delayed change in gene expression describe different aspects of biological activity. Comparing time courses can reveal differences that a single measurement would miss. The ultimate goal is to connect observations with appropriately limited explanations, not to infer clinical outcomes from isolated molecular signals.
