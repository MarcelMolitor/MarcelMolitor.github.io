1. Beyond classical cannabinoid receptors

The endocannabinoidome includes related lipids, enzymes and molecular targets. Not all targets are classical cannabinoid receptors.

2. GPR55

Lysophosphatidylinositol can elicit GPR55-associated responses. Classification as a third cannabinoid receptor is not generally established.

3. TRP channels

TRPV1 and TRPA1 are ion channels, not classical GPCRs. Anandamide can activate TRPV1 under certain conditions.

4. From signal to hypothesis

Experiments should document receptor expression, concentration-response relationships and controls. Calcium responses alone do not prove selectivity or clinical efficacy.

5. Selectivity and signaling

A ligand may affect multiple targets. A testable question specifies a ligand, target, endpoint and comparison conditions.

6. A receptor name does not establish a treatment

Cell models establish findings within their experimental context. Clinical evaluation requires evidence of exposure, safety and meaningful outcomes.

7. GPR55: multiple measurable responses

LPI-associated ERK phosphorylation and RhoA-dependent calcium responses illustrate different levels of cellular signaling.

8. Receptor selectivity as a research problem

Concentration, receptor density and assay design influence interpretation. Calcium measurements alone cannot distinguish direct channel activation from indirect GPCR signaling.

9. From cell response to clinical evidence

Target binding, cellular responses, organism-level exposure and clinical outcomes are distinct levels of evidence.

10. Molecular communication

Receptors translate molecular interactions into cellular responses. Cells differ in their available signaling machinery and biological functions.

11. Receptor dynamics

Prior activation may alter subsequent responsiveness. Desensitization, phosphorylation, arrestin interactions and receptor trafficking are related but distinct processes.

12. Functional pharmacology and pathway selectivity

Relative pathway responses depend on reference ligands, receptor expression, assay sensitivity and timing. A single endpoint cannot establish signaling bias.

13. PPAR and gene regulation

PPARs are nuclear receptors that regulate transcription, typically through heterodimers with RXR. Related lipid mediators such as OEA and PEA are studied in relation to PPAR alpha, without implying exclusive mechanisms.
