1. A biological communication system

The ECS comprises endogenous lipid messengers, receptors and enzymes. It influences synaptic transmission and immune functions, among other processes. It is not a single organ.

2. CB1 and CB2

CB1 is particularly widespread in the nervous system, while CB2 is found in immune cells, among other locations. Neither distribution is exclusive. Both are G protein-coupled receptors. Their activation can inhibit adenylyl cyclase and influence ion channels.

3. Anandamide and 2-AG

Anandamide and 2-AG are produced through different metabolic pathways. FAAH primarily degrades anandamide, while MAGL accounts for much of 2-AG degradation. Retrograde signaling can modulate presynaptic neurotransmitter release.

4. What remains unknown

Receptor expression alone does not demonstrate therapeutic benefit. Dose, cell type and disease stage may alter outcomes. Research question: what controls would establish that an effect is mediated by CB1?

5. Interpreting receptor findings

Binding affinity and functional efficacy are distinct properties. A cell experiment can help identify a mechanism but cannot establish its relevance in a living organism at achievable concentrations. Consider receptor expression, concentration dependence, cell viability and independent tests of receptor dependence.

6. Time and location of signaling

Endocannabinoid signals are often generated on demand. A postsynaptically produced messenger can reach presynaptic CB1 receptors and alter transmitter release. Bulk tissue concentrations do not necessarily reveal local signaling strength at an individual synapse.

7. Interpreting evidence

Original studies of retrograde communication investigate specific neuronal mechanisms. They do not establish that generally increasing ECS activity benefits humans. Reviews provide context but do not replace appraisal of primary experiments.

8. 2-AG as a dynamic feedback mechanism

At many synapses, postsynaptic DAGLα generates 2-AG, which can reduce transmitter release through presynaptic CB1 receptors. Genetic mouse studies by Tanimura and Gao support the importance of DAGLα; developmental adaptation remains a possible confounder. Pharmacological experiments in brain slices complement genetic evidence. These findings do not establish clinical efficacy. Hypothesis: selectively altering DAGLα activity should change the time course of a defined feedback mechanism.

9. From molecules to networks

Reducing transmitter release at excitatory synapses may decrease downstream activity, whereas reducing release at inhibitory synapses may promote disinhibition. The network outcome depends on connectivity. Research exercise: which measurements could distinguish presynaptic changes from altered postsynaptic responsiveness?
