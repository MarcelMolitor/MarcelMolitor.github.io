1. The intestinal barrier

Epithelium, mucus, immune cells and microbiota form a dynamic barrier. Endocannabinoid signaling may influence motility and immune responses in a context-dependent manner.

2. TNF and interleukins

These signaling proteins have distinct functions. CB2-mediated changes in immune cells may affect cytokine responses, but do not establish a general anti-inflammatory effect in humans.

3. Gut and brain

Neural, hormonal and immune signals connect the gut and brain. The ECS is one component of this network.

4. A testable hypothesis

A defined cell model could test whether CB2 modulation changes TNF release, with controls for viability, concentration and receptor specificity.

5. Measurable endpoints

Electrical resistance and marker permeability capture different aspects of epithelial barrier function. Neither alone establishes clinical benefit.

6. Animal models and clinical reality

Animal findings provide mechanistic clues. Human studies illustrate why symptoms and objectively measured inflammation must be assessed separately.

7. Barrier function as a research endpoint

Epithelial permeability does not measure overall gut health. Ligand identity and experimental context matter when interpreting cannabinoid effects.

8. Symptoms and clinical endpoints

Symptoms, quality of life and objective inflammation are distinct outcomes. A research table should separate these outcomes, adverse events and uncertainty.

9. Architecture of the intestinal interface

The intestinal surface must absorb nutrients while limiting uncontrolled passage of luminal contents. Mucus, epithelial junctions and immune cells contribute different functions. Electrical resistance and molecular permeability are complementary measurements.

10. The gut-brain axis

Neural, endocrine, immune and metabolic communication can affect gut function. Reduced pain does not necessarily mean reduced tissue injury.

11. Microbiome and lipid mediators

Microbial composition and metabolic activity are distinct. Associations between microbial profiles and host endocannabinoids require mechanistic testing and control of confounding factors.

12. Immune cells and resolution of inflammation

Resolution is an actively regulated process. Macrophage states depend on context; reduced cytokine release alone does not demonstrate restored tissue function.
